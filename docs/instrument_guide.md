# Decision guide: bonds or loans, in dollars or in pesos?

**For:** corporate banking and fintech analysts · **Base:** 42 corporate bonds (ON) from Pampa, YPF and Vista (2021-2025), their loans at year-end 2025
and financial-system rates. It describes what these three companies did; it is not a rule for any issuer.

## When each one fits

### For the company

- **Hard-dollar bonds, when country risk falls.** They were issued with country risk from 597 points. In 2025, hard-dollar bonds
  priced at 7.9% and 7.2 years on average, 289 points below that day's country risk on average.
- **Dollar-linked bonds, when country risk is high.** None was issued below 1,555 points. They pay almost nothing in dollars
  (1.8% on average) because local investors want currency protection, but they are short (5.2 years) and settle in pesos.
  The model estimates a 58% probability of choosing them at 2,000 points and 3% at 700.
- **Bank loans, for short tenors or amounts too small for a bond.** At year-end 2025, Vista owed US$ 688 M
  to banks, with tenors of 2.5 years at a fixed rate and 5.0 years over SOFR, while its bonds reached 8 years.
- **Export pre-financing, for an exporter that needs dollar working capital.** It is tied to export receipts, so banks see it as less risky
  than a plain loan. Pampa used it and repaid it within 2025.
- **Pesos, only for short working capital.** In 2025, peso lending to companies cost 43% on average, against
  6.2% in dollars. These companies barely borrow in pesos: 0.8% of YPF's debt and none at Pampa and Vista.

### For the bank

- **Underwriting is recurrent:** Balanz and Banco Galicia took part in all 6 placements in the sample. A local bond gathers
  15 placement agents on average: the bank that places does not take the credit risk, it distributes it among investors and keeps the relationship.
- **Lending stays short:** with a dollar lending rate of 6.2% and dollar time deposits at 1.4% in 2025,
  the gross margin was about 4.9 points. For longer tenors, the company prefers a bond.
- **In an international bond, the local bank only distributes:** the largest placement in the sample, Vista ON XXIX (US$ 900 M), had foreign investment banks
  as initial purchasers and only 4 local placement agents.
- **What to check before lending:** the maturity wall and how much of it cash covers. With its cash, Pampa covers maturities until
  2033; YPF does not cover 2026.

### For a fintech

- **It does not lend to these companies:** their size and tenors are beyond what a fintech competes for.
- **It distributes bonds to retail investors:** digital platforms took part in 3 of the 5 local bonds in the sample,
  all from 2025. It is how savers reach a corporate bond.
- **It uses them to invest its own or its clients' cash:** a 2025 hard-dollar bond yielded 6.6 points more than a dollar
  time deposit, in exchange for tenor and credit risk. What to measure is the same as a bank: rating, maturities and covenants.

## Limits

- These are three large companies in the same sector. A small company has no access to international bonds and depends much more on banks.
- The placement-agent sample covers 6 of 42 bonds: those with a results notice or prospectus supplement available.
- The system rate is an average for non-SME companies at a fixed rate: a reference, not these companies' cost.
